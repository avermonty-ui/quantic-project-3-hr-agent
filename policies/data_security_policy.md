# Data Security and Acceptable Use Policy

## Purpose

This policy explains how employees must protect company data, customer data, employee data, devices, accounts, and systems. All employees are responsible for following security requirements when working onsite, remotely, or while traveling.

## Company Devices

Employees should use company-approved devices to access company systems. Company devices must be configured with required security software, encryption, screen lock, and endpoint protection.

Employees may not disable security tools, share devices with unauthorized users, or store company data on unapproved personal devices.

## Authentication

Employees must use strong passwords and multi-factor authentication for company systems. Passwords must not be shared with coworkers, managers, family members, or external parties.

Employees must report suspected account compromise immediately.

## Remote Work Security

Employees working remotely must use secure internet connections and company-approved tools. Public Wi-Fi should be avoided unless the employee uses company-approved security controls such as VPN or equivalent protections.

Employees may not access company systems from public computers, hotel business centers, shared kiosks, or borrowed devices.

## Confidential Information

Confidential information includes employee records, customer information, financial data, strategy documents, source code, security information, contracts, and non-public business information.

Employees should only access confidential information when needed for their job duties.

## Data Sharing

Company data may only be shared through approved systems. Employees may not forward company documents to personal email accounts, upload confidential files to unapproved cloud storage, or share access links with unauthorized parties.

## Lost or Stolen Devices

Employees must report lost or stolen company devices immediately to IT and their manager. The report should include the employee name, device type, last known location, approximate time of loss, and whether company data may have been exposed.

## Phishing and Suspicious Activity

Employees must report suspected phishing emails, suspicious links, unexpected login prompts, or unusual system behavior. Employees should not click suspicious links or open unexpected attachments.

## Security Incidents

A security incident may include unauthorized access, accidental disclosure, lost devices, malware, phishing, or improper sharing of confidential information. Security incidents must be reported immediately.

## Policy Violations

Violations of this policy may result in loss of system access, disciplinary action, or additional training. Serious violations may require HR, Legal, IT, or management review.
